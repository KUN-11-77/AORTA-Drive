# R004H4P2R1 comparable-speed candidate-supply replay

## Decision

**STOP — the original constant-acceleration/constant-curvature provider has inadequate
intent supply after correcting speed semantics.** Exactly 82 of 128 fit anchors
(`0.640625`) had at least one fully eligible candidate; the minimum group fraction was
`0.5`. These are below the unchanged adequacy requirements of `0.90` overall and `0.75` in
every group.

## Controlled comparison

The replay changed only the speed observable. Both native and challenger used rear-axle
displacement over `[3.5,4.0] s` divided by `0.5 s`. Anchors, provider controls, current-speed
inputs, eligibility budgets, ordering and decision thresholds were identical to R004H4P2.
Supply rose from the invalid-interface value 26/128 to 82/128.

Across 4,256 candidate evaluations, the exhaustive sequential ledger contained 246
eligible candidates, 2 terminal-speed failures after earlier gates, 383 cross-track
failures, 75 heading failures, 778 excess-progress failures, 2,632 insufficient-progress
failures and 140 evaluations from four anchors with insufficient native displacement.
The payload-free mechanical audit replayed all counts and passed.

## Failure structure and implication

Of the 46 unsupplied anchors, the candidate nearest the registered progress interval failed
cross-track on 14, progress below on 12, progress above on 11, heading on 5, and the native
minimum-displacement definition on 4. Exactly 23 already had zero progress gap. This rules
out a one-dimensional acceleration-grid repair: half of the unresolved anchors require a
better route-conditioned lateral/heading family, while the four low-progress anchors are
undefined by design and must not be forced into eligibility.

The result authorizes one physically structured provider revision on fit data only. It does
not authorize threshold relaxation, geometry claims, development access, coverage, Gate B
or H4 registration. External cross-model review remained unavailable.
