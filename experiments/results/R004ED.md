# R004E-D Result — Development visual-necessity control

## Verdict

**FAIL. Calibration remains sealed.** The frozen epoch-1 visual checkpoint beat the
train-label mean spatial prior, but it was substantially worse than a zero-difference
field on both preregistered recording-group endpoints.

| System | Worst-5 group mean | All-24 group mean |
|---|---:|---:|
| Zero-difference field | `1.000000954 m` | `1.000000725 m` |
| Train-label mean spatial prior | `1.771293473 m` | `1.751649211 m` |
| Frozen visual checkpoint | `1.475400233 m` | `1.394614086 m` |

The visual model is about `47.5%` worse than zero on the primary endpoint and `39.5%`
worse on the secondary endpoint. The tiny difference between the C2 and replayed
visual numbers is within the already qualified runtime materialization tolerance and
does not affect the verdict.

## Theoretical implication

For a 1-Lipschitz signed-distance teacher sampled on 1 m graph edges,

\[
|(D\widetilde d)_e|\lesssim 1\ \mathrm m.
\]

Therefore the zero predictor has the deterministic edge-residual control

\[
|0-(D\widetilde d)_e|\lesssim1\ \mathrm m,
\]

which is exactly reflected by the measured control. The current unconstrained visual
potential can create edge gradients whose error exceeds this trivial bound. A learned
model that cannot beat zero under the exact score used for calibration has not
justified opening calibration or claiming useful uncertainty geometry.

This does not prove visual gradients are intrinsically unlearnable. It narrows the
next falsification question to where the excess residual comes from: output-gradient
overshoot, edges incident to all-missing nodes, or wrong-sign/poorly generalized
gradients even on observed edges.
